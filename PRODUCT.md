# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Sports bettors, prediction-market analysts, and technically comfortable operators use Sports Projector while researching live and historical betting lines. They are trying to inspect public sports data, compare projection outputs against market or sportsbook-style lines, and understand whether a line is supported by the model evidence.

## Product Purpose

Sports Projector is a read-only research workspace for sports projections, public market context, and local NBA model artifacts. It helps users inspect live and historical projections, compare those projections to public market or sportsbook-style lines, and identify advised lines only when the assumptions, confidence, and caveats are visible beside the result.

Success means the product makes the reasoning inspectable: current score state, model inputs, historical context, public market context, confidence, and limitations are clear enough that a user can audit the projection instead of trusting a black box.

## Brand Personality

Precise, analytical, restrained.

The voice is calm and evidence-led. It can be direct when a line is advised, but it should never overstate certainty or sound like a tout service.

## Anti-references

Avoid casino aesthetics, sportsbook hype, neon gambling visuals, fake certainty, and anything that makes the product feel like a tout service. Avoid decorative clutter, especially sports graphics that do not help a user understand state, interaction, or projection updates.

## Design Principles

1. Evidence before advice. Any advised line must sit next to the inputs, assumptions, confidence, and caveats that support it.
2. Restraint builds trust. Use density, clear labels, and quiet hierarchy instead of hype language or visual spectacle.
3. State changes should show work. Sports and data graphics belong in transitions, refresh states, selected games, loading states, and projection updates, not as decoration.
4. Keep the workspace inspectable. Users should be able to move from a game to a projection to model context without losing source, status, or caveat information.
5. Never imply certainty the model does not have. Confidence and uncertainty are product features, not disclaimers to hide.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA where practical. Preserve strong keyboard support, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, readable contrast, and status language that does not rely on color alone for live, error, success, or advised-line states.
